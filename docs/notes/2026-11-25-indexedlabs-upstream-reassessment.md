Try sharing a regression and a store fix upstream, then see whether both projects can agree on the durable delivery and recovery contract that would make upstream adoption worthwhile.

## Engine findings

### Timestamp predicates and session settings

**Confirmed shared correctness gap, high priority where timestamp predicates are used.** The local engine represents dates and timestamps as `Value::Text`, compiles raw literal text and compares text lexically. PostgreSQL can print a cell as `2026-10-19 16:00:00+00` while the application supplies `2026-10-19T07:00:00Z`. The first string sorts below the second because of the space versus `T`, even though the cell is later. Snapshot SQL evaluates the actual timestamps, so a snapshot can contain a row whose later changes the engine excludes. Session defaults also influence time-zone and date formatting. Our `pg::connect` configures a connection timeout and makes the connection without pinning either setting. [Local predicate comparison](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/predicate.rs#L506), [local text comparison](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/predicate.rs#L581), [local connection setup](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/pg.rs#L37).

Upstream #69 pins query and replication startup options to UTC and ISO dates, verifies query sessions accepted them, canonicalizes supported scalar timestamp/date literals at compilation, covers equality/template and circuit-key paths, and refuses unsupported timestamp literal forms. The final code is substantially simpler than intermediate revisions that normalized decoded cells. Port the final contract and hostile-session-default tests. Our `pgwire-replication` dependency is already 0.5.0, while upstream deliberately uses 0.4.1. Adapt the current API and qualify backpressure/reconnect behavior, without importing its dependency downgrade. Existing shapes whose keys or values were printed differently require recreation. [PR #69](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/pull/69), [final commit](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/commit/b42f70d0b1489dd72feb7b5f2ee8ae71259bb169), [literal implementation](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/blob/9bc1563f5718db7bc502eeb79388ee189f1fd5f9/apps/engine/src/pg_temporal.rs), [local dependency](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/Cargo.toml#L62).

### Snapshot-only rows can survive a changes-only subquery feed's move-out

**Confirmed shared correctness gap in the subset/subquery feed surface.** A changes-only feed skips its backfill and initializes its delivered-key set empty. Our `SubqueryRegistry::emit_for_shapes` sends a delete only when that key was in the feed's own set. A client can already hold the row from a separate subset snapshot. A soft delete, window move, hard delete or inner membership revocation can then remove it from the query without the feed ever delivering its removal. The local source has both sides of this mismatch. Its actual application exposure depends on whether that consumer opens this changes-only feed path. [Local empty seed](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/engine/lifecycle.rs#L1899), [local delete gate](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/subquery.rs#L2027).

Upstream #70 carries prior-membership proof from the old row or an inner-set flip, reconstructing pre-commit membership while a bounded proof record remains available. Its own PR explicitly records incomplete cases when a later commit arrives before deferred query-back work, or the proof record exceeds 8 MiB. Two membership causes in one transaction can still leave a snapshot row present until the next snapshot in those cases. Use its regressions and diagnosis to choose a complete contract for our supported surface. The Circuits subset harness exercises changes-only feeds. The stock pgxsinkit control plane passes through an explicit `changesOnly` request, so the impact here depends on the caller choosing that mode. [PR #70](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/pull/70) · [Merged commit](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/commit/e10c4bd893e7ed847aa269195fa9ef34d8daf8c1) · [pgxsinkit request compiler](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/pgxsinkit/blob/74a3d8abc7cf5b1211f3a8d7675df14b39a72510/packages/server/src/circuits/compile.ts#L297)

### Lost change-log segments can leave readiness active

**Confirmed shared availability gap under external deletion or storage loss, with no newly reproduced local incident.** Local startup checks the restored starting segment and resolves the current segment. It does not HEAD each intermediate segment required to replay between them. A missing intermediate segment can therefore pass boot. During live replay a definitive missing-stream error logs an error and backs off forever. That branch leaves the engine reporting readiness while it cannot advance. [Local start-segment check](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/engine/mod.rs#L1593), [local missing-segment loop](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/engine/sequencer.rs#L1172).

Upstream #35 adds a crash/fault harness and withdraws readiness for this condition. #39 checks the complete required chain at boot and requests restart on a terminal live GET failure. Its title refers to confirming GET with HEAD, but its final body and source say that approach was rejected. The implemented policy lets a false 404 cost a restart, then verifies the chain at boot. Adapt to our fail-closed sequencer policy and typed storage outcomes. Test both real loss and a transient false response before choosing whether automatic restart is appropriate. Importing the fault-schedule coverage is independently valuable, although its in-memory store does not model filesystem fsync/torn-write durability. [PR #35](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/pull/35), [PR #39](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/pull/39), [upstream crash harness](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/blob/9bc1563f5718db7bc502eeb79388ee189f1fd5f9/apps/engine/src/engine/crash_consistency_tests.rs).

### Boot can queue application reads behind an unnecessary exclusive lock

**Confirmed shared operational gap.** Local boot executes `ALTER TABLE … REPLICA IDENTITY FULL` for every tracked table, including those already FULL, before introspection. That ALTER takes `ACCESS EXCLUSIVE`. A long open transaction can block the ALTER and queue later application readers behind it. Our drift helper has a bounded lock wait, but boot calls the separate unbounded helper. [Local boot](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/engine/mod.rs#L1475), [local boot and drift helpers](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/pg.rs#L625).

Upstream #72 introspects first, skips the ALTER for FULL tables, bounds the wait to two seconds for the rest, introspects again after changing identity and retries if a concurrent migration changed it back. This is a bounded repair with real PostgreSQL lock-test coverage upstream. Preserve our fingerprint/replica-identity drift checks and SQLSTATE retry handling. [PR #72](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/pull/72), [merged change](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/commit/5b2cf3e2731d7fcecc3f95b9c05a5963b9c6b363).

### The sequencer retains an entire incomplete transaction in RAM

**Confirmed shared resource gap.** Our ingestor can spill and our pending-shape queues share a spill budget, but the sequencer independently accumulates incomplete input in `Vec<Envelope>`. At each next page it joins the held vector to the newly read envelopes. A large committed transaction can therefore consume its whole decoded size there even when the ingestor and storage pages are bounded. Pending-buffer accounting does not cover that vector. [Local held vector](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/engine/sequencer.rs#L507), [page merge and trailing hold](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/engine/sequencer.rs#L775), [local pending budget](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/pending_buffer.rs#L88).

Upstream #32 adds a held-transaction spill representation, initially with a 64 MiB threshold, and one admission ledger across large payload stages. It also includes static ingest column projection, managed-source features and Electric folding. Extract held spill first with transaction-boundary, duplicate-prefix, failed-processing rewind, checkpoint highwater and shutdown tests. A broader shared payload budget is a separate design since retained arrangements/results still fall outside upstream's ledger. Our existing pending/spill design should be reused where its semantics fit. Upstream reports production OOM incidents as motivation, which establishes its deployment experience without measuring our workload. [PR #32](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/pull/32), [merged commit](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/commit/224cd1c7bebdae525e1bdda7eea77a5a5619c184), [upstream held transaction](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/blob/9bc1563f5718db7bc502eeb79388ee189f1fd5f9/apps/engine/src/engine/held.rs).

### Unrelated subquery creates block behind one initializer

**Confirmed shared performance limitation.** Our cancellation repair gives `CreateGuard` an owned engine-wide `Mutex<()>` admission guard. It holds that guard across inner seeds, outer backfill and installation or completed compensation. This correctly preserves single-owner initialization, but an unrelated slow seed delays all subquery creates. Our newer chunked inner seeding reduces the retained row cost compared with upstream's earlier design, while the global exclusion remains. [Local engine-wide mutex](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/engine/mod.rs#L236), [local owned admission](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/engine/lifecycle.rs#L2109), [chunked seed](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/engine/lifecycle.rs#L1874).

Upstream #41 reserves canonical membership signatures atomically, releases reused-node reservations before outer I/O and separately bounds fresh initializer sets with a configurable default of four. Its regressions hold a PostgreSQL relation lock and require unrelated creates to finish, then test N+1 fresh creates and cancellation while cleanup is blocked. Adapt the ownership and admission contract to our chunked installer. This is a useful throughput improvement after correctness fixes. [PR #41](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/pull/41), [merged commit](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/commit/82c77935f8240c8e3c77f8a90c734432186525c0).

### One membership flip repeats the same PostgreSQL read for many dependents

**Confirmed shared scaling limitation.** Local `propagate_one` walks each edge and invokes a fresh query-back for each shape or parent node. Many scopes sharing an outer table and connecting column consequently repeat the same candidate SELECT, including full table reads for relevant NULL flips. [Local edge loop](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/subquery.rs#L2308), [per-shape query](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/subquery.rs#L2397).

Upstream #67 groups dependents by connecting column and target table, reads once for each group and evaluates each dependent with its own recency fence. It releases the registry lock between dependents and balances in-flight accounting on empty results/errors. Indexed Labs reported a production flip reaching 445 shapes during a burst of roughly 1.6 million queries in 23 minutes. #67 is suitable for adaptation with the lost-flip, concurrent-live-update and retry tests. Open #68 extends this to coalesced rounds for bulk flips and merits monitoring after qualification. [PR #67](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/pull/67) · [Merged commit and incident report](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/commit/1be10fb8313af3247c5dbc8b3190d355419c4c79) · [Open PR #68](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/pull/68)

### Subquery output lacks commit positions

**Confirmed wire difference, consumer-dependent impact.** Local subquery upserts pass `None` for `headers.lsn` and deletes have no position argument. Upstream #71 attaches the originating transaction and a nondecreasing per-stream placement to live, replayed and query-back output. Deferred query-backs can finish after a newer source commit, so blindly stamping the triggering LSN would make later authoritative output look older to a client that compares positions. Our harness subset client uses positions. Production pgxsinkit deduplicates on opaque durable offsets, so adding these headers alone offers it little benefit. A synthetic placement LSN cannot prove that all source effects through that LSN have landed. [Local output construction](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/circuits/blob/3e526d88ab5e64d6051fcbbb664d14db11caeb9a/apps/engine/src/subquery.rs#L2053) · [PR #71](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/pull/71) · [pgxsinkit convergence ADR](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/pgxsinkit/blob/74a3d8abc7cf5b1211f3a8d7675df14b39a72510/docs/adr/0056-catchup-alignment-on-stream-offsets.md) · [Local unsequenced-output ledger](../backlog/0034-unsequenced-output-needs-separate-restart-qualification.md)

### Why the current upstream binary cannot replace ours

- **Plain Postgres shapes require conditional, committed append receipts.** Our engine checks for `Stream-Seq-Guard` and POSTs its intended `Stream-Seq` with `Stream-Expected-Seq` to fence late writers and suppress effects already delivered. Upstream implements an unconditional sequence comparison and has no such guard. Our engine rejects the missing capability. Standard producer counters do not provide the same proof after a crash. [Local engine check](../../apps/engine/src/ds.rs#L447) · [Upstream append](https://github.com/indexedlabs/durable-streams-rust/blob/42eff1b2d604a995d9706cfec5f4366f90ca1007/src/handlers.rs#L1663) · [Delivery ADR](../adr/0012-plain-shape-delivery-frontiers-survive-restart.md)
- **A fresh volume and a different deployment are required for a pilot.** The local WAL writes kind 5 for sequenced appends. Upstream decodes kinds 1–4, so a retained local WAL is an unsupported downgrade. Upstream WAL mode also requires an existing store manifest, configured shard/lane and filesystem identity, an artifact digest and explicit opt-in for a non-loopback bind. It provides a bootstrap command for empty directories. [Local codec](../../apps/durable-streams/src/wal/codec.rs#L45) · [Upstream codec](https://github.com/indexedlabs/durable-streams-rust/blob/42eff1b2d604a995d9706cfec5f4366f90ca1007/src/wal/codec.rs#L45) · [Upstream startup](https://github.com/indexedlabs/durable-streams-rust/blob/42eff1b2d604a995d9706cfec5f4366f90ca1007/src/main.rs#L620)
- **Upstream still contains mechanisms we repaired locally.** It captures the checkpoint tail without our append staging/rollback fence, lacks our release-mode recovery guards for later WAL gaps, and copies live append fields into full metadata saves. Local semantic tests caught these classes in October. This comparison identifies present source mechanisms, with upstream runtime behavior still untested here. Convergence needs those tests or equivalent guarantees. [Upstream tail capture](https://github.com/indexedlabs/durable-streams-rust/blob/42eff1b2d604a995d9706cfec5f4366f90ca1007/src/wal/shard.rs#L1030) · [Upstream recovery](https://github.com/indexedlabs/durable-streams-rust/blob/42eff1b2d604a995d9706cfec5f4366f90ca1007/src/wal/recovery.rs#L250) · [Local metadata repair](../backlog/0029-general-metadata-writers-capture-speculative-append-state.md) · [Local checkpoint repair](../backlog/0030-checkpoint-tail-can-cross-a-rejected-append.md)

## Re-adoption assessment

The original ownership decision assumed the two upstreams would see little further maintenance. That premise needs updating for Indexed Labs. They are shipping fixes with real workload evidence, maintaining CI, and publishing commit-tagged container images. The other reasons for owning the stack still apply: one qualified pair, a small native surface, and control of the recovery contracts. The comparison is against the Indexed Labs forks of our former repositories, the line of development relevant to this decision. [Circuits ADR-0011](../adr/0011-one-repository-no-longer-a-fork.md) · [pgxsinkit ADR-0065](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/pgxsinkit/blob/74a3d8abc7cf5b1211f3a8d7675df14b39a72510/docs/adr/0065-own-the-circuits-stack.md) · [Engine image build](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/actions/runs/45342415530) · [Store image build](https://github.com/indexedlabs/durable-streams-rust/actions/runs/45255810873)

### Engine integration

- **A native control-plane foothold exists.** The upstream `/shapes` routes still take our predicate AST and named leases. The create response still has `shapeId`, `subscription`, `leaseSeconds` and `streamPath`, and `/replication/lsn` still exposes the three fields our client reads. This makes a bounded compatibility spike plausible. The production reader uses opaque stream offsets, so tests must cover rows, retirement and stale-read refusal rather than just comparing JSON schemas or checking for LSN headers. [Upstream native HTTP](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/blob/9bc1563f5718db7bc502eeb79388ee189f1fd5f9/apps/engine/src/http.rs#L392) · [pgxsinkit engine adapter](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/pgxsinkit/blob/74a3d8abc7cf5b1211f3a8d7675df14b39a72510/packages/server/src/circuits/engine-client.ts#L85)
- **The engine and edge disagree about the physical stream path.** Upstream returns the logical `shape/<id>` in `streamPath`, and builds `streamUrl` with the physical prefix `circuits/v1/<namespace>/stores/<generation>/queries/<generation>/`. Our edge authorizes the returned path and appends it to the configured store base. The upstream production build also checks store identity and readiness before any ordinary traffic and binds catalog event zero to that identity. A trusted, scope-aware edge mapping or upstream change to the native response is needed, along with manifest and namespace initialization. A clean deployment with a dedicated slot/publication and store namespace is the candidate migration path. Reusing existing catalogs, filesystem volumes or client offsets would need an explicit migration design and proof. [Upstream shape response](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/blob/9bc1563f5718db7bc502eeb79388ee189f1fd5f9/apps/engine/src/http.rs#L1601) · [Upstream path contract](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/blob/9bc1563f5718db7bc502eeb79388ee189f1fd5f9/apps/engine/src/store_identity.rs#L110) · [Upstream bound catalog](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/blob/9bc1563f5718db7bc502eeb79388ee189f1fd5f9/apps/engine/src/engine/catalog.rs#L1250) · [pgxsinkit edge](https://github.com/pgxsinkit/pgxsinkit/blob/74a3d8abc7cf5b1211f3a8d7675df14b39a72510/packages/server/src/circuits/edge.ts#L294)
- **The harder gap is existing behavior.** Our schema-digest replay fence, durable retirement, and original seed/registration plus per-shape append receipt must pass the same adversarial cases on any replacement. Upstream improved the *global* checkpoint floor in #34, but still lacks Circuits' conditional, per-shape committed frontier. Its #71 stamps positions for a different consumer contract. A fresh store resolves format differences and leaves this behavioral gap. The upstream engine also carries Electric compatibility, shape groups, managed sources and ownership/handoff work. We could keep those routes private, and would still depend on their implementation and release path. [Circuits ADR-0010](../adr/0010-change-log-envelopes-carry-their-schema-and-processing-fails-closed.md) · [Circuits ADR-0012](../adr/0012-plain-shape-delivery-frontiers-survive-restart.md) · [Upstream #34](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/pull/34) · [Upstream #71](https://github.com/indexedlabs/electric-circuits/pull/71)
- **Published images make a pilot practical.** The upstream engine and log-server Docker jobs both built the audited SHAs. The engine repository's bundled DS build and default harness still pin crates.io `0.1.5`, so successful upstream CI alone cannot qualify those two current builds together. Its CI qualifies PostgreSQL 18, as does Circuits today. Our PG19 deployment target needs a real logical replication, schema, restart and full-stack qualification lane for whichever pair we select. The current pgxsinkit Compose default is an older Circuits pair (`sha-4c48e83`) and supports image overrides, so record the candidate digests and a same-workload control before drawing migration conclusions. ### Recommendation

| Option | Assessment |
| --- | --- |
| Keep Circuits and exchange fixes with Indexed Labs | Best next move. We can take production-tested design and regression cases where contracts match and contribute our own crash-consistency findings. Revisit the decision when we know which shared contracts the maintainers want to own. |
| Adopt Indexed Labs Durable Streams independently | The cleaner eventual component boundary. Viable once the upstream project accepts a committed conditional append contract and the storage recovery cases that Circuits depends on. Otherwise we would still own a long-lived storage patch series. |
| Adopt the current Indexed Labs engine and store together | A credible candidate for a clean, isolated experiment and a material migration for production. Worth pursuing only with agreement on supported native leases, delivery/restart semantics, edge path mapping and PG19 coverage. An integration repository could still pin and qualify the pair without owning both implementations. |
| Maintain a wholesale rebased mirror of the upstream engine | Low payoff. Both sides changed catalog/restore and storage semantics, and much of the new engine surface supports their app. Rebasing all local changes would repeatedly re-resolve those contracts and restore the ownership cost the move avoided. |

For a small collaboration trial, pick a shared engine regression such as timestamp bounds or missing segment handling and one portable store patch such as linear recovery. Share our append-receipt and recovery tests first as a design discussion. A shared upstream becomes attractive if the maintainers agree to keep those contracts in their mainline, and real pgxsinkit tests stay green against pinned upstream artifacts. Activity confirms a source of fixes and workload evidence. The project history alone cannot establish willingness to maintain our requirements.

If that agreement is promising, qualify a clean pair with pgxsinkit's existing edge, registry and client on PostgreSQL 19. Exercise subscribe/renew/release, projection and path authorization, atomic snapshot/live handoff and subquery revocation, and actual byte/offset behavior under lost append acknowledgement, store restart, engine restart, and schema drift. Compare query-back counts, restart time, backlog and publish-to-client latency on our workload. Keep the incumbent deployment as rollback while clients resubscribe and rehydrate from Postgres.
